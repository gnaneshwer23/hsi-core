## Budgets, replay and dead letters

Job creation is unique on user/organization/idempotency key and rejects a changed plan. Durable quota policy hashes cannot silently change when a second connection opens the store. UTC fixed-window per-user/organization job and reservation quotas share the coordinator. A user cannot reset their quota by selecting another organization. Investigation caps are hash-locked before trial. Maximum input/output tokens and integer cost micro-units are reserved atomically before external dispatch, with user/org and investigation checks in the same transaction. A denied reservation produces no provider callback. The actual P0 transport must enforce output/response/deadline bounds and the approved pricing/reservation calculation; checking actual usage after completion cannot undo an overcharge.

Each operation has a stable ID, bounded attempts, a lease and a monotonic fence. Completed checkpoints are reused. A verified retry is allowed only when the previous receipt independently proves **zero input/output tokens and zero charge**, or the coordinator proves that the provider execution callback was never invoked (for example, a transient source-authority outage), and the retry policy permits it. Explicit authorization denial is quarantined rather than retried. Do not label an HTTP 429/5xx response as zero-charge without provider-specific proof. Unknown completion/usage is **null**, not zero. Reservations remain charged conservatively against caps until independently reconciled. There is no universal exactly-once guarantee for an external provider; store uniqueness is not a vendor idempotency promise.

If a worker crashes after persisting `started`, restart quarantines it as unknown and blocks automatic replay. Stale fences cannot settle or create decisions. A separately verified completion receipt can advance the checkpoint without dispatching again. Provider/source outages, invalid/unverified receipts, exhausted retries and bounds breaches produce blocked/dead-letter work, retained traces and alerts. Inspect the operation and provider journal before any operator redrive. No public endpoint automatically empties or replays the dead-letter queue. A changed source/prompt/model plan requires a new signed version and explicit decisions about reused work and possible extra charges. Logs/checkpoints may not fabricate an absent provider receipt.

Provider/job/write switches are independent and default off. Workers check current switches before dispatch and poll during an active step; abort requests cannot undo an already completed provider action, so in-flight work becomes unknown rather than a promised refund. The worker does not perform graph publication. The operations `writesEnabled` flag does not replace existing Neon write guards; a live deployment must couple its operator kill procedure to actual API maintenance, Neon write guard and P0 provider service. No production switch is enabled here.

## Restore, rollback and release packet

The test suite exercises two real SQLite connections, a real worker subprocess killed after a durable start, fencing/reconciliation, and a consistent `VACUUM INTO` backup reopened with checkpoints, quota/idempotency state and immutable decisions intact. This is **local coordinator recovery evidence only**, not a full Neon/vault/platform restore or production RPO/RTO demonstration. Never raw-copy a live WAL database as a complete backup. Off-host backup verification, restore-to-isolated-target, integrity/receipt comparison, encryption/access, scheduled recovery points and measured restore duration remain required. An old backup must not reset charge/invitation/decision history: freeze dispatch on restore, reconcile the provider journal and external decision ledger before reopening. The backup routine disables job/provider/write switches inside the snapshot and marks it recovery-required. Reopening it cannot enable dispatch until independently verified restore reconciliation clears that flag; switches remain off afterward until a separate authorized activation. Unapproved raw copies do not have this guarantee and must never be used to bypass the recovery gate.

Rollback rehearsals must pin the last accepted artifact **and configuration**, preserve forward-compatible checkpoint/decision/budget state, stop new admission/provider/writes first, verify authenticated reads and reverse the deployment/config only under the signed runbook. A public static artifact rollback does not restore database integrity. No production rollback or infrastructure restore is performed here.

`verifyPilotReleasePacket` binds exact tested commit SHA, deployable artifact digest, configuration, quota policy, invitation cohort and the proposed-objective hash. It requires purpose-bound Ed25519 signatures from three distinct configured security/scientific/operations owners, current expiration and independent verification of every P0/P1, soak, alert, outage/restart, duplicate-charge/decision, restore, rollback, kill, isolation/invitation and human-review evidence gate. A merge SHA must not substitute for an untested artifact; identical source trees alone do not prove an identical deployable build. CI must supply a trusted build/test attestation for the exact promoted digest. No signing key or real release signature is fabricated or committed.

The verifier has no deployment/invitation side effects and cannot grant scientific approval or graph promotion. Only after the packet passes and accountable operators explicitly activate the accepted artifact/config/cohort may invitations start. Every material brief needs independent scope-appropriate human review of its exact artifact hash; the job cannot become completed from transport success alone. Store decisions bind the final immutable output hash and are unique under concurrency/retry. A brief decision never transfers graph publication approval. Every graph promotion uses its separate governed human decision. Capture review corrections, completed investigations, abandonment, measured cost and researcher usefulness before considering broader access.
